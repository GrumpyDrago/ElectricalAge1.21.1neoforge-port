Besides the classic batteries there are four of real chemistries:

| | Lead-acid | Nickel-cadmium | Lithium-ion | LiFePO4 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Voltage | sloping | very flat | sloping | flattest |
| Power | 1.5× | 2× | 2× | 3× |
| Energy | 1.8× | 1.3× | 3× | 3× |
| Life | 1.5× | 4× | 2× | 6× |

(Multiples of the cost oriented battery.) All are made for 50 V; put them in series for more.

## Wear

Batteries wear only with use, more at high current. Heat makes that wear faster (twice as fast for every 10 °C above
25 °C), lead-acid wears when run flat, and lithium cells are damaged by charging below freezing. A battery that sits
unused keeps its life. The cold also raises their resistance, so they give less power in winter.

## Overheating

A battery pushed too hard, or overcharged, does not explode: it **vents**. It cuts itself off, glows red, smokes and
loses a tenth of its life. Use a **water bucket** on it to cool it and bring it back, or break and replace it. A
battery that turns reddish and smokes a little is close to its limit: give it less current.

Lithium-ion cells above 80 °C heat themselves faster and faster (thermal runaway) until they vent. Keep them cool.
