# Breaker boxes

A breaker box is a house's consumer unit: one supply in, eight circuits out, each with its own breaker, all in one
**power bundle cable**.

| | 50 V | 200 V | 800 V |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Each line | 20 A | 10 A | 6.25 A |
| Whole box | 8 kW | 16 kW | 40 kW |

1. Feed the front of the box with the **supply cable** of its voltage; an ordinary cable only carries one line's worth.
2. Run the bundle of the same voltage from its back. Bundles join line for line, but no machine connects to them.
3. Take a power cable out of the bundle where you need a circuit. Its brush color picks the line: **black**
   (unpainted), **red, orange, yellow, green, blue, purple** or **white**.

In the box's screen, switch breakers on and off and set their ratings (shift-click to lower). A breaker trips within
five seconds on an overload and at once on a short circuit; its red light shows it. Switch it on to reset it.

Fed by a box, a bundle never overheats. Used as a plain multi-core cable, without a box, its lines share its heat.

## Lockout/tagout

Before working on a circuit, use a **lockout padlock** on the breaker, switch, box or substation that feeds it. It
opens, and nobody can close it, by hand, by signal or by computer, until every lock is off. Only you can take your
lock off, by using a padlock on it again.
