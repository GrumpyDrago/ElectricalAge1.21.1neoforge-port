# The basics

**Voltage** (volts, V) is the push; **current** (amps, A) is how much flows; **power** (watts, W) is the two
multiplied. A 50 V battery giving 20 A gives 1000 W.

Every cable has a **resistance**, which turns some of the power into heat. The loss grows with the square of the
current: twice the current, four times the heat. That is why power travels far at a high voltage and a low current.

## The voltages

| Voltage | Cable | For |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 50 V | Low Voltage Cable | small machines, batteries, solar |
| 200 V | Medium Voltage Cable | most machines |
| 800 V | High Voltage Cable | big machines, links between buildings |
| 3.2 kV | Very High Voltage Cable | long runs, power stations |

Transformers and DC-DC converters change one voltage into another. A machine fed more than about 1.3 times its
voltage burns out; a cable carrying more than its current overheats and burns.

## Heat

Parts heat up as they work, and the **ambient temperature** of the biome and the depth adds to it: deep underground
or in a desert, parts run hotter. Jade and the thermometer show temperatures; the thermal camera shows them all at once.
